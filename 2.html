01 / Core rules

The whole lecture, in the facts that matter

No paragraphs to re-read — just the rules and numbers examiners actually ask about.

Cell Envelope

  • Cytoplasm → wrapped by the cytoplasmic membrane → wrapped by the cell wall (peptidoglycan).
  • Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan, no outer membrane.
  • Gram-negative: thin peptidoglycan + periplasmic space + outer membrane.
  • Capsule (when present) = anti-phagocytic, found in some species of both types.

Cytoplasmic Content

  • Most bacterial chromosomes: single, circular, double-stranded DNA.
  • Plasmids = smaller extra-chromosomal circular DNA; not essential, but often carry antibiotic resistance.
  • Cytoplasm also holds mRNA, ribosomes, and proteins.

Reproduction

  • Asexual — Binary fission: no spindle fiber; cell doubles, replicates chromosome, wall forms a septum → 2 daughter cells.
  • Asexual — Budding: a few bacteria & yeasts; bubble-like growth separates from parent.
  • Gene transfer — Conjugation: cell-to-cell contact via sex pili.
  • Gene transfer — Transformation: uptake of free DNA from the medium.
  • Gene transfer — Transduction: gene transfer via a virus.

Growth Curve — 4 Phases

  • Lag: no rise in numbers; high metabolic prep activity.
  • Log (exponential): rapid doubling each generation time.
  • Stationary: numbers plateau; cells shrink; triggered by overcrowding, waste build-up, nutrient/O₂ loss.
  • Death: slow die-off, caused by loss of intracellular ATP.

Physical Growth Factors

  • pH: Acidophiles 1–5.4 (e.g. Lactobacillus) · Neutrophiles 5.4–8.5 (most pathogens) · Alkaliphiles 7.0–11.5 (e.g. V. cholerae).
  • Temp: Psychrophiles 15–20°C · Mesophiles 25–40°C (body 37°C) · Thermophiles 50–60°C · Hyperthermophiles >80°C.
  • O₂: Obligate aerobe (M. tuberculosis) · Facultative anaerobe (E. coli) · Obligate anaerobe (N. meningitidis) · Microaerophilic (Campylobacter).
  • Only spores survive a dry environment; UV/gamma rays mutate or kill cells.

Chemical Growth Factors

  • O₂ & H₂ come from water — essential for growth.
  • Autotrophs: carbon from CO₂ · Heterotrophs: carbon from organic sugars.
  • Inorganic ions needed: N, S, P, Mg, K + trace elements.
  • Organic needs: carbohydrates (energy), amino acids, trace vitamins/purines/pyrimidines.
